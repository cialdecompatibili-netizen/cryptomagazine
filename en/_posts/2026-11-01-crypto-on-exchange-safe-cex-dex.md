After the **$6.3 billion** theft from Binance ([news report]({{ '/en/blog/news/binance-hack-6-billion/' | relative_url }})) many people wonder whether it still makes sense to leave crypto on an exchange. There is no single answer, but the facts of this case help. This article is informational and is not financial advice.

## What happened to users

Binance said it will cover all losses with its SAFU emergency fund and company funds. It set aside more than $6.34 billion, of which $5 billion was already held in the same stolen tokens, and is buying the rest. It said it intends to publish a full reserves report and is working with the FBI. It also said there is no evidence that wallet private keys were compromised.

So the real cost to users, at least so far, has mainly been **access**: withdrawals were paused for roughly two days while the exchange reworked its systems. These are company statements, updated hour by hour: check the official announcements.

## The "DEXs are better" argument

A Standard Chartered analyst argued the hack could push more trading toward decentralized exchanges (DEXs), citing Uniswap as an exchange that has never been exploited. According to CoinGecko the DEX-to-CEX volume ratio among the top ten was already 23.5% in the second quarter of 2026.

But "decentralized" does not mean "risk-free". During the attack, for example, the lending protocol Aave froze its markets as a precaution after the attacker borrowed about $44.6 million: Aave's contracts were not compromised, but the episode shows how interconnected the systems are. DeFi protocols have also been hit: investigator ZachXBT was first to flag a likely $10 million exploit on THORChain, later confirmed by security analysts. And on a DEX you are the one protecting the funds: if you lose your keys or sign something malicious, nobody reimburses you.

## Two models, two risks

- **Centralized exchange:** convenient, with support and, in this case, a fund covering losses. The risk is that you do not control the keys: if the exchange is attacked or freezes withdrawals, you depend on it.
- **Personal wallet (self-custody):** the keys are yours and nobody can lock you out, but mistakes are final: lost keys, phishing scams, signing malicious transactions.

## Precautions that always apply

1. **Do not keep more on the exchange than you need** to trade.
2. Turn on two-factor authentication with an app or physical key, not SMS, and the withdrawal address whitelist if available.
3. For amounts you will not need soon, consider a **hardware wallet**, writing the recovery phrase down offline and never in a photo or the cloud.
4. Be wary of messages asking you to "secure" your funds after an attack: scammers exploit exactly these moments.
5. Check the exchange's official channels and reserve reports, remembering they are company statements.

## The market after the attack

In the hours of the attack Bitcoin fell below $77,000, down about 4.9% in 24 hours, while BNB lost about 8%. The Fear and Greed index fell to 30, "fear". These are snapshots that change.
