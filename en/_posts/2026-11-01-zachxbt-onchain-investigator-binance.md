After the roughly **$6.3 billion** theft from Binance ([our news report is here]({{ '/en/blog/news/binance-hack-6-billion/' | relative_url }})), the name that comes up most among investigators is **ZachXBT**. He is often called a "hacker", but it is the opposite: he follows stolen funds on the blockchain and publishes the evidence. This article explains who he is, what he said about the Binance case and why his analyses are taken seriously, separating facts from his own claims.

## Who he is

ZachXBT is a pseudonymous on-chain investigator. His method is easy to describe: on public blockchains every transfer is visible, so anyone who can read addresses, timing and amounts can link one theft to another and sometimes trace who is behind it. His work rests on public data that anyone can check. In 2023 a former target of his investigations sued him and exposed his identity, but he kept working.

Some milestones, according to Decrypt:

- **2024:** his tracing of the theft of about 4,100 BTC from a Genesis creditor helped lead to arrests.
- **February 2025:** Paradigm hired him as an incident-response advisor. Co-founder Matt Huang said he had already helped return more than $350 million to victims.
- **Funding:** he says he works thanks to foundation grants and individual donations.

## The Bybit case: proof within hours

On 21 February 2025, the day of the Bybit theft (about $1.4-1.5 billion), ZachXBT submitted on-chain evidence of the Lazarus Group's involvement. The FBI confirmed the attribution days later. Arkham paid him a bounty of 50,000 ARKM tokens, about $34,000. In the following days he also tied the wallets used to launder those funds to earlier thefts at Phemex and BingX.

## The disguise: posing as a client

The most striking part is an investigation he described on 5 October 2026. By his account, after Bybit he noticed accounts asking for help in public Telegram and Discord groups with orders tied to stolen funds. He presented himself as a client of a Chinese criminal organization that, he says, works for North Korea, fronted $349,700 of his own money and accepted losing 5% on each order to earn trust.

The result, again by his account: he located a cluster of more than $12 million of Bybit loot being moved in real time, and Tether later froze 442,000 USDT linked to it. He also says he has helped action more than $75 million in freezes tied to North Korean incidents since 2022. These are his own statements as reported by Decrypt; we have not seen independent verification of every figure.

## What he said about Binance

On 30 October 2026 he published a simple, weighty finding: funds stolen from Binance and from the earlier Bitget theft (about $388 million) arrived at the same address after passing through **THORChain**, a cross-chain swap service. In his view this indicates the same actor is likely behind both. He recalled that the Bitget attack had been attributed to North Korea's TraderTraitor, with overlaps with other thefts such as KelpDAO.

The same day TRM Labs and Chainalysis also attributed the attack to TraderTraitor. Precision matters here: these are attributions based on evidence, phrased as "likely" and "high confidence", not a verdict. Binance, for its part, said its investigation has not attributed responsibility to any individual.

## Why it matters

A multi-billion-dollar theft looks like a perfect heist, but on a public blockchain every move leaves a trace. The work of people like ZachXBT gives stablecoin issuers and exchanges what they need to **freeze funds in time**: in the Binance case about $3 billion was frozen.
